This exercise is a workshop for mid level to senior leaders in the NHS as part of the HSMA lambda series of workshops.

We assume that most of the audience have limited to no exposure to data science, and likely even less to operational research concepts.

They are often working in organisations with no data science capacity, or a relatively immature data science facility.

The purpose of the workshop is to equip them with enough knowledge to commision new projects using data science/OR for service improvement, and critically interpret the outputs. We teach two other standalone workshops on discrete event simulation, and machine learning for operational improvement.

This is a spin-off from the Health Service Modelling Associates programme, which teaches the 'doing' part of this work to analysts, clinicians, etc.


## Flow

In the first part of the session, participants are introduced to

- the HSMA programme, its impact, and how HSMA lambda differs
- the difference between operational research and data science
- What AI and machine learning are
- classification problems
- basic pros and cons of machine learning
- HSMA projects using machine learning
- Features
- Training sets
- feature weighting
- overfitting
- underfitting
- performance metrics: accuracy, precision, recall
- tree based models vs neural networks

They then undertake an exercise where they manually build a simple algorithm for predicting whether a DVD should be selected for a given person.

In the section that is delivered directly before the exercise being created into this repository, students will be introduced to:
- ML models are black boxes - we can create powerful predictors but we often don’t understand why they make those predictions
- Intro to Shap values and waterfall plots
- Causality vs explainability
- Other performance measures they might need to interpret - confusion matrices
- Converting probability to predictions

After this section, students will see slides on
- what could go wrong?
- representation in training sets
- transparency in training datasets
- real-world consequence examples of poorly trained or poorly designed AI models

Students will conclude the day by coming up with operational healthcare ML project ideas for students on the HSMA programme to undertake.
